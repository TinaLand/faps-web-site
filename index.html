Failure-Aware Policy Shielding

Detecting and correcting risky internal states in frozen vision-language-action policies.

FAPS adds an inference-time safety layer around SmolVLA. It reads intermediate transformer activations, predicts near-term failure, and injects a low-rank residual into the action expert before the final action is produced.

Research Question

Can a robot policy be rescued without retraining it?

Vision-language-action policies can fail under camera blur, action noise, and joint noise. Instead of fine-tuning the whole model, FAPS asks whether we can detect risky internal states and apply a small activation-level correction during inference.

What counts as success?

  • Detect risky steps before task failure.
  • Avoid false alarms on nominal episodes.
  • Produce nonzero residuals that stay on the activation manifold.
  • Improve closed-loop LIBERO success rate under perturbations.

System Design

Three modules around a frozen policy.

FAPS treats SmolVLA as fixed infrastructure. The learned pieces sit outside the base model and use forward hooks to read and edit hidden states.

01

Risk head

Cross-layer fusion and a temporal transformer convert SmolVLA features into a risk probability, time-to-failure prediction, and embedding z_t.

02

Hysteresis gate

The shield turns on only after risk exceeds delta_high and turns off after K low-risk steps below delta_low. This avoids trigger chatter.

03

Flow residual

Flow matching predicts a correction in 48-D PCA space. The correction is injected into a chosen action-expert transformer layer.

Why internal activations?

Action-space patching happens after the policy has already committed. Hidden-state injection gives downstream layers a chance to revise the plan before the final 7-D robot action is emitted.

Why PCA?

Direct 480-D edits are high variance and can leave the model's training manifold. PCA keeps corrections low-rank and aligned with common SmolVLA activation directions.

Why conceptors?

Conceptors summarize success, recovered, and failure activation subspaces. They provide pseudo-targets for flow residuals when true recovery deltas are not directly supervised.

Data And Labels

We turn rollouts into step-level failure supervision.

The project uses LIBERO rollouts with nominal behavior, continuous perturbations, and windowed perturbations. The windowed setting is important because the policy gets a chance to recover after the perturbation turns off.

Label audit lesson

A strong offline AUC can be misleading if the detector never sees nominal successes as clean negatives. The next risk-head retrain explicitly includes nominal-success episodes to reduce false alarms on perturbation=none.

Experiment Design

Each phase answers a different question.

We separate detection, correction, and closed-loop behavior so that a good detector score is not confused with a working recovery system.

Phase 1

Risk-head ablation

Question: Which supervision makes a useful detector and embedding?

Runs: A BCE, B BCE+TTF, C BCE+TTF+NT-Xent, D NT-Xent only.

Decision: Use B as pure detector baseline; use C as downstream encoder.

Phase 1b

Calibration and threshold sweep

Question: Which trigger threshold fires early without too many false alarms?

Outputs: reliability diagrams, temperature scaling, threshold_sweep.json.

Decision: Use calibrated hysteresis settings for shield eval.

Phase 2

Flow matching

Question: Can we learn meaningful activation residuals?

Runs: B-family recipe tests, C-layer sweep, D/Z controls.

Decision: C12/C13 and B5 are the main candidates for online eval.

Phase 3

Closed-loop shield eval

Question: Does FAPS improve LIBERO success rate?

Metrics: success rate, trigger rate, false alarm, recovery success.

Status: current result is diagnostic; final eval follows retrain.

Operational Details

How we actually ran the project.

The project became feasible after moving heavy training to Modal, archiving W&B outputs, and copying NPZ data onto local SSD inside jobs to avoid repeated network-volume reads.

Speedups that mattered

Lazy NPZ loading avoided loading every feature file up front. Modal volume to local NVMe copy avoided slow network reads. DataLoader workers, pin_memory, and persistent_workers improved GPU utilization during risk-head training.

What we archive

Each finished run should have the exact command, Modal logs, W&B summary, plots, checkpoint path, and a short interpretation. This makes ablations auditable rather than just a collection of run names.

Visual Evidence

Different figures answer different claims.

The main mistake to avoid is treating every plot as proof of recovery. Detector plots, calibration plots, flow plots, and closed-loop results support different pieces of the story.

Phase 3 Diagnostic

Closed-loop recovery is the final test.

The current online shield result used the pre-retrain Run C risk head and layer-11 injection. It is useful because it revealed the real bottleneck: false-positive triggers can over-inject and hurt otherwise recoverable episodes.

The final plan is to use a nominal-aware risk head, run B5 layer-11 smoke, sweep thresholds, then compare B5, C12, and C13 with the same hysteresis setting.

Current lesson

FAPS should not be judged by trigger rate alone. A useful shield must trigger on perturbations while staying quiet on nominal or already-safe recovery states.
