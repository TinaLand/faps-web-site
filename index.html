Abstract

We study whether a frozen vision-language-action policy can be made safer without fine-tuning its weights. SHIELD / FAPS is a triggered, learned, safety-metriced activation-space shield: it fires only when calibrated risk crosses a threshold, injects a flow-model correction conditioned on failure context, and falls back to the unmodified policy when inactive. Across LIBERO perturbation rollouts, risk is strongly detectable from activations and Run C provides the best downstream representation geometry. The latest B6 layer-11 shield rescues harder action-noise tasks, improving task 3 from 30% to 60% and task 4 from 80% to 90%. Easier high-baseline tasks show the central limitation: false-positive triggering can make unnecessary correction worse than staying quiet.

Contributions

  • Triggered risk gate over frozen SmolVLA activations.
  • Joint BCE, TTF, and NT-Xent training for risk and geometry.
  • Learned PCA-constrained flow residual conditioned on z_t.
  • Safety metrics beyond success rate: false alarms, trigger precision, recovery.

Research Question

Can a robot policy be rescued without retraining it?

Vision-language-action policies can fail under camera blur, action noise, and joint noise. Instead of fine-tuning the whole model, FAPS asks whether we can detect risky internal states and apply a small activation-level correction during inference.

What counts as success?

  • Detect risky steps before task failure.
  • Avoid false alarms on nominal episodes.
  • Produce nonzero residuals that stay on the activation manifold.
  • Improve closed-loop LIBERO success rate under perturbations.

Positioning Against Prior Work

FAPS is not just another always-on steering method.

The poster's strongest framing is that FAPS combines three pieces that are usually studied separately: failure detection, activation steering, and safety-aware closed-loop evaluation.

Activation steering

RepE, ITI, ROME, and MEMIT show that hidden states can be edited in large language models. FAPS brings the idea to continuous-action robot policies, where an edit must still produce a valid 7-D action.

VLA robustness

VLA-OS, Don't Blind the VLA, LAE, Cerebellar, and ReSteer study layer analysis, action-space correction, and steerability. FAPS adds a calibrated trigger so the correction is not always on.

COAST

COAST uses always-on closed-form conceptor steering. FAPS instead asks when to intervene, learns a flow residual, and evaluates safety behavior under adversarial and windowed perturbations.

System Design

Three modules around a frozen policy.

FAPS treats SmolVLA as fixed infrastructure. The learned pieces sit outside the base model and use forward hooks to read and edit hidden states.

01

Risk head

A 3.76M-parameter SharedEncoder uses temporal CNN features, cross-layer attention over six SmolVLA layers, and temporal context to output risk, TTF, and z_t in R^256.

02

Hysteresis gate

The shield enters when risk reaches 0.7 and exits after K=5 steps below 0.4 in the current diagnostic setting. Threshold sweep chooses the final operating point.

03

Flow residual

Flow matching predicts z in R^48, projects it through the top-48 PCA basis U_k, and injects Delta = U_k z into a chosen action-expert transformer layer.

Why internal activations?

Action-space patching happens after the policy has already committed. Hidden-state injection gives downstream layers a chance to revise the plan before the final 7-D robot action is emitted.

Why PCA?

Direct 480-D edits are high variance and can leave the model's training manifold. PCA keeps corrections low-rank and aligned with common SmolVLA activation directions; the compact 48-D basis keeps about 95% of the relevant variance in the poster analysis.

Why conceptors?

Conceptors summarize success, recovered, and failure activation subspaces. They provide pseudo-targets for flow residuals when true recovery deltas are not directly supervised.

Data And Labels

We turn rollouts into step-level failure supervision.

The project uses 5,117 labeled LIBERO rollouts across 11 perturbation types, with nominal behavior, continuous perturbations, and windowed perturbations. Each trajectory is converted into step-level fail_in_H labels with horizon H=30 plus time-to-failure and coarse failure-type metadata.

Experiment Design

Each phase answers a different question.

We separate detection, correction, and closed-loop behavior so that a good detector score is not confused with a working recovery system.

Phase 1

Risk-head ablation

Question: Which supervision makes a useful detector and embedding?

Runs: A BCE, B BCE+TTF, C BCE+TTF+NT-Xent, D NT-Xent only.

Decision: Use B as pure detector baseline; use C as downstream encoder.

Phase 1b

Calibration and threshold sweep

Question: Which trigger threshold fires early without too many false alarms?

Outputs: reliability diagrams, temperature scaling, threshold_sweep.json.

Decision: Use calibrated hysteresis settings for shield eval.

Phase 2

Flow matching

Question: Can we learn meaningful activation residuals?

Runs: B-family recipe tests, C-layer sweep, D/Z controls.

Decision: C12/C13 and B5 are the main candidates for online eval.

Phase 3

Closed-loop shield eval

Question: Does FAPS improve LIBERO success rate?

Metrics: success rate, trigger rate, false alarm, recovery success.

Status: Post-retrain layer-11 shielding improves action noise, but false-positive triggering remains the bottleneck.

Speedups that mattered

Lazy NPZ loading avoided loading every feature file up front. Modal volume to local NVMe copy avoided slow network reads. DataLoader workers, pin_memory, and persistent_workers improved GPU utilization during risk-head training.

Phase 3 Diagnostic

Closed-loop recovery is the final test.

The latest online shield result uses the retrained Run C risk head and B6 layer-11 conceptor-flow injection. The clearest signal is not universal improvement; it is selective recovery. On harder action-noise tasks, FAPS improves task 3 from 30% to 60% and task 4 from 80% to 90%. On easier tasks where the unshielded policy is already strong, extra intervention has little room to help and can hurt.

The next lever is trigger quality: stricter thresholds, persistence, or cooldowns should reduce false positives before we scale to broader C-layer and E-subspace comparisons.

Current lesson

The residual has recovery signal. The unresolved problem is deciding when to use it. A useful shield must trigger under real policy stress while staying quiet on nominal or already-safe recovery states.

Phenomenon

FAPS helps most when SmolVLA is genuinely under stress. Hard action-noise tasks show recovery gains, while easy high-baseline tasks leave little room for intervention to help.

Why it matters

The residual can move activations toward recovery, but an eager gate can also perturb a rollout that was already on a successful path. This turns trigger precision into the main system bottleneck.

Next step

Sweep stricter hysteresis thresholds, require more consecutive high-risk steps, add cooldowns, and select the operating point by success rate under a false-positive cap.

Poster Takeaways

The main scientific story in three claims.

These are the parts worth borrowing from the poster because they make the project feel like a research argument instead of only a run log.

Risk is accessible in VLA activations.

BCE+TTF reaches wAUC 0.927, so the frozen policy's hidden states already contain enough signal to rank near-term failure.

Contrastive geometry is a design choice.

Run C gives up only about 0.010 wAUC relative to B, but improves silhouette from -0.040 to +0.168, making z_t more useful for flow.

The online bottleneck is calibration.

B6 layer-11 can rescue hard action-noise failures, but the main remaining failure mode is triggering too often on safe or already-recovering states.

What We Would Do Next

Turn the diagnostic into final evidence.

1. Reduce trigger false alarms

Sweep higher delta thresholds and longer k-consec persistence after the retrained nominal-aware risk head.

2. Add trigger guardrails

Add cooldowns or stricter persistence so the shield keeps the action-noise gain without over-injecting on clean states.

3. Run final shield table

Report unshielded vs FAPS success rate, trigger precision, false alarm, and recovery success across perturbation types.

4. Compare intervention subspaces

Test U_k versus action-sensitive U_potent and null-space U_null, then compare first-step, last-step, and always-on injection timing.

5. State limitations clearly

Current evidence is simulation-only, uses one SmolVLA policy, and still needs broader C-layer/E-subspace evaluation.
